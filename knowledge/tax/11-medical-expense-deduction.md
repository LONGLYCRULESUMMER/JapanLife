# Medical Expense Deduction (医療費控除)

If you or your household paid significant medical costs during the year, you can deduct part of
them by filing a tax return. This often produces a refund for families and anyone with a hospital
stay or ongoing treatment.

## Threshold and Limit

You can deduct medical expenses that exceed **¥100,000** in the calendar year (or **5% of your
income** if your income is below about ¥2 million), up to a maximum deduction of **¥2,000,000**.

The amount you deduct is reduced by any reimbursements you received, such as insurance payouts or
the High-Cost Medical Expense Benefit (高額療養費).

## What Counts

Qualifying costs include:

- Doctor, dentist, and hospital treatment fees.
- Prescription medicines.
- Necessary medical transport (e.g., ambulance, taxi when medically required).
- Some costs of childbirth and certain medical devices.

Not deductible: cosmetic procedures, general health tonics/supplements, and most preventive costs
that are not treatment.

## Self-Medication Tax System (セルフメディケーション税制)

As an alternative, the self-medication system lets you deduct spending over **¥12,000** on certain
switch-OTC drugs (up to ¥88,000), if you also undertake a health check. You must choose **either**
the regular medical expense deduction **or** the self-medication system — not both.

## How to Claim

- You must file a **tax return (確定申告)**; this deduction is not available through year-end
  adjustment.
- Attach a **Medical Expense Statement (医療費控除の明細書)** summarizing the costs (you no longer
  attach every receipt, but keep receipts for **5 years**).
- A health-insurance "medical expense notice" can be used to help complete the statement.

## Tips for Foreigners

1. Combine the **whole household's** medical costs and claim them under one filer (often the
   higher earner).
2. Subtract insurance reimbursements before applying the ¥100,000 threshold.
3. Keep receipts for five years in case of review.
