# Tax When Leaving Japan (出国時の税金)

Leaving Japan does not end your tax obligations automatically. Two issues catch people out: your
final income tax return, and resident tax that is billed in arrears.

## File Before You Leave, or Appoint a Tax Representative (納税管理人)

If you leave Japan partway through the year and have a duty to file (for example, freelance income
or income not fully settled by year-end), you have two options:

1. **File a tax return before departure**, settling that year's income to the point you leave; or
2. **Appoint a Tax Representative / Tax Agent (納税管理人)** — a person resident in Japan who files
   and pays on your behalf the following spring. You submit a notification to the tax office before
   you leave.

## Resident Tax (住民税) Lag

Resident tax is billed the year **after** the income year, based on where you lived on **January 1**.
This means you can owe resident tax even after you have left Japan. Handle it by:

- Paying the remaining balance in a **lump sum** before departure (often deducted from your final
  salary), or
- Leaving it to your **tax representative** to pay the installments.

## Pension Lump-Sum Withdrawal (脱退一時金)

If you paid into the pension system and leave Japan, you may claim a **lump-sum withdrawal payment**
after departure (subject to limits on the number of contribution months). A withholding tax of
**20.42%** may apply, part of which can often be reclaimed through your tax representative.

## Final Administrative Steps

- Submit a **moving-out notification (転出届)** at the ward office.
- Settle or transfer **National Health Insurance** and **National Pension**.
- Close or settle local accounts and confirm your final resident-tax position.

## Tips for Foreigners

1. **Appoint a tax representative before you leave** if anything remains unsettled — it is hard to
   arrange from abroad.
2. The **resident-tax timing** surprises many people; budget for a bill that arrives after you go.
3. Keep documents for the **pension lump-sum** and tax reclaim.
