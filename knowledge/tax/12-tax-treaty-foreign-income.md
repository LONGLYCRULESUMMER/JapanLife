# Tax Treaties and Foreign Income (租税条約・国外所得)

How much of your worldwide income Japan taxes depends on your **tax residency status**, and tax
treaties can reduce double taxation. This is one of the most confusing areas for newcomers.

## Tax Residency Categories

- **Resident (居住者)** — has a domicile (住所) in Japan or has lived here for one year or more.
- **Non-Permanent Resident (非永住者)** — a resident without Japanese nationality who has had a
  domicile/residence in Japan for **5 years or less within the last 10 years**.
- **Non-Resident (非居住者)** — everyone else; taxed only on Japan-source income.

## What Gets Taxed

- **Permanent residents (tax sense)** are taxed on **worldwide income**.
- **Non-permanent residents** are taxed on Japan-source income, plus foreign-source income that is
  **paid in Japan or remitted to Japan**.
- **Non-residents** are taxed only on Japan-source income.

## Foreign Tax Credit (外国税額控除)

If the same income is taxed both abroad and in Japan, the foreign tax credit lets you offset
foreign tax paid against your Japanese tax, reducing double taxation. Keep proof of foreign tax
paid.

## Tax Treaties (租税条約)

Japan has tax treaties with many countries that can reduce or eliminate withholding on items like
dividends, interest, royalties, and certain student/researcher income. To benefit, you usually
file an **Application Form for Income Tax Convention (租税条約に関する届出書)** with the payer
before payment.

## Reporting Overseas Assets (国外財産調書)

Residents who hold more than **¥50 million** in overseas assets at year-end must file an overseas
assets report. There is also an assets-and-liabilities statement for high-income filers.

## Tips for Foreigners

1. Track **how many years** you have been in Japan — it changes what income is taxable.
2. Keep evidence of **foreign tax paid** for the foreign tax credit.
3. If a treaty applies to you (e.g., students, researchers), submit the treaty notification early.
