# Resident Tax (住民税)

## What Resident Tax Is
Resident tax (住民税) is a local tax made up of a prefectural portion (都道府県民税) and a
municipal portion (市区町村民税). It funds local services and is **separate from national income
tax (所得税)**, which goes to the National Tax Agency.

## How It Is Calculated
Resident tax has two parts:
- **Income-based portion (所得割)**: roughly **10%** of your previous year's taxable income
  (generally 4% prefectural + 6% municipal).
- **Per-capita portion (均等割)**: a flat amount of about **¥5,000** per year (including the
  ¥1,000 national Forest Environment Tax collected together since 2024).

Because it is based on the **previous calendar year's income**, the amount is fixed by where you
lived and what you earned last year.

## Timing: Why Your First Year Is Different
- Resident tax for a given year is billed starting in **June**, based on income from January to
  December of the **prior** year.
- People in their **first year** in Japan usually pay **no resident tax**, because there was no
  prior-year Japanese income. The bill arrives the following June.
- Likewise, if you stop working you may still owe resident tax the next year on the prior year's
  income.

## How You Pay
- **Special collection (特別徴収)**: deducted from your monthly salary from June through May of the
  next year (12 installments). This is the default for employees.
- **Ordinary collection (普通徴収)**: you pay yourself using payment slips (納付書), usually in
  **four installments** (June, August, October, January). This applies to the self-employed and to
  people who left a job.

## Leaving Japan
If you leave Japan partway through the year, you may need to pay your remaining resident tax in a
lump sum or appoint a **tax representative (納税管理人)** to handle it after you go.
