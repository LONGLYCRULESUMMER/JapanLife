# Salary Income vs Business Income

## Salary income

Salary income generally comes from an employment relationship. The employer controls working conditions, pays wages, withholds income tax, issues a Certificate of Income and Tax Withheld, and may handle year-end adjustment. Employees usually do not deduct ordinary work expenses directly, because salary income uses statutory salary-income calculations.

## Business income

Business income generally comes from independently operated work, such as freelancing or a sole proprietorship. The person invoices clients, bears business risk, manages expenses, and keeps accounting records. Business income is normally reported on a final tax return, and eligible business expenses are calculated from records.

## Side income

Side income is not classified only by size. It may be salary, business income, miscellaneous income, or another category depending on the actual facts. Occasional small projects can be treated differently from continuous independent activity with multiple clients and business-like operations.

## Practical classification tips

Look at the substance: who controls the work, whether tax is withheld, whether you invoice, whether you bear costs, and whether the activity is organized and continuous. Keep contracts, invoices, payment records, and expense records so the classification can be explained if asked.
