# Permanent Residency Failure Reasons

Permanent residency applications are reviewed against residence history, conduct, livelihood, and
public-interest factors. This guide is general information, not legal or immigration advice.

## Common Eligibility Issues

Applications can fail when the residence period is too short, the applicant has not spent enough time
in Japan, the current status is unstable, or the applicant does not meet a special shortened route
such as spouse or highly skilled professional criteria.

## Money and Public Duties

Immigration often reviews income stability, household support, taxes, pension, health insurance, and
other public obligations. Late, missing, or inconsistent payments can be a major weakness even when
income is otherwise sufficient.

## Records and Documents

Traffic violations, criminal penalties, missed immigration notifications, unexplained long absences,
weak guarantor documents, or inconsistent application evidence can lead to refusal or requests for
additional materials.

## Cautions

A refusal does not automatically mean permanent residency is impossible later. Identify the reason,
correct what can be corrected, and build a cleaner record before reapplying. Do not submit documents
that are inaccurate or incomplete.
