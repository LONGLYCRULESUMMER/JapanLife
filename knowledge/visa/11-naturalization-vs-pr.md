# Naturalization (帰化) vs Permanent Residency (永住)

Long-term residents often weigh two paths: permanent residency, which keeps your nationality, and
naturalization, which makes you a Japanese citizen. They are handled by different authorities and
have different requirements.

## Permanent Residency (永住)

- You **keep your current nationality** and passport.
- You still hold a **residence card** and remain a foreign national.
- You can work in any field with no status restrictions.
- You **lose the status** if you stay abroad too long without a re-entry permit.
- Typically requires about **10 years** of continuous residence (shorter for spouses of Japanese
  nationals and Highly-Skilled Professionals).
- Decided by the **Immigration Services Agency**.

## Naturalization (帰化)

- You **become a Japanese citizen** and receive a Japanese passport.
- Japan generally **requires renouncing your previous citizenship**.
- Typically requires about **5 years** of continuous residence, basic Japanese-language ability,
  stable livelihood, and good conduct.
- Decided by the **Legal Affairs Bureau (法務局)** under the Ministry of Justice.

## Comparison

| | Permanent Residency | Naturalization |
|---|---|---|
| Nationality | Keep yours | Become Japanese |
| Document | Residence card | Japanese passport / koseki |
| Voting rights | No | Yes |
| Re-entry permit | Required | Not required |
| Authority | Immigration | Legal Affairs Bureau |
| Revocable | Yes (status) | No (citizenship) |

## Which to Choose

Choose **permanent residency** if you want to keep your nationality and the flexibility of dual
ties to your home country. Choose **naturalization** if you want full citizenship, voting rights,
and to travel on a Japanese passport.

## Tips for Foreigners

1. Naturalization paperwork is **heavier** and includes interviews and document gathering.
2. PR holders **still need re-entry permits** when leaving Japan.
3. Confirm whether your home country allows giving up citizenship before pursuing naturalization.
