# Reporting Changes to Immigration (届出義務)

Medium- and long-term residents have a legal duty to report certain changes to the Immigration
Services Agency. Missing these notifications can hurt future renewals and permanent-residency
applications.

## Notify Within 14 Days

You must notify immigration **within 14 days** when:

- You **change or leave your employer / affiliated organization** (for work and student statuses).
- Your organization changes **name or location**, or is dissolved.
- For status based on a spouse (e.g., Spouse of Japanese National, Dependent), you **divorce** or
  your spouse **passes away**.

Changes to your **name, date of birth, sex, or nationality** are also reported.

## Address Changes Go Through the Ward Office

When you move, register your new address at the **ward/city office** with a moving-in (転入届) or
moving-within-city (転居届) notification **within 14 days**. This municipal registration feeds your
address to immigration — you do not file address changes with immigration separately.

## How to Notify

- **In person** at a regional immigration office,
- **By mail** to the Tokyo Regional Immigration Services Bureau, or
- Online via the **Electronic Notification system (電子届出)**.

Have your **residence card** ready.

## Consequences of Not Reporting

Failure to report can lead to **fines**, and it is viewed negatively when you apply to **renew** or
seek **permanent residency**. Repeated or long-overdue failures can affect your status.

## Tips for Foreigners

1. The **employer-change notice** is the one people forget most — do it within 14 days.
2. Keep **copies** of every notification and the receipt.
3. Moving house? The ward-office address registration covers the immigration side.
