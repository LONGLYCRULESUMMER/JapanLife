# Visa Types for Foreign Residents in Japan

Japan grants a **status of residence (在留資格)** that defines what activities you may do and how
long you may stay. Below are the statuses foreign residents encounter most often.

## Work Visa: Engineer/Specialist in Humanities/International Services (技術・人文知識・国際業務)

The most common work status, covering engineers, IT, marketing, translation, and many
office/specialist roles. It requires a **job offer matching your degree or relevant experience**.
Validity is typically **1, 3, or 5 years** and is tied to working for an employer in the approved
field.

## Highly-Skilled Professional (高度専門職)

A points-based status for high earners and advanced professionals. Reaching 70 points grants a
5-year stay and a **fast track to permanent residency** (1–3 years). See the dedicated
Highly-Skilled Professional guide.

## Permanent Residency (永住)

Permanent residency (永住権) generally requires about **10 continuous years** in Japan, with at
least **5 years on a work status**. Spouses of Japanese nationals and Highly-Skilled Professionals
may apply sooner. You must show stable income and tax/pension payment records.

## Dependent Visa (家族滞在)

For the **spouse and children** of work-status holders. Dependents may work **up to 28 hours/week**
only after obtaining **permission to engage in activity other than that permitted (資格外活動許可)**.

## Student (留学) and Designated Activities (特定活動)

The **Student (留学)** status is for those enrolled at a school or university; students may work up
to 28 hours/week with the activity permit. **Designated Activities (特定活動)** covers special
cases such as job-hunting after graduation and certain internships.

## Specified Skilled Worker (特定技能)

Created to address labor shortages in specific sectors (e.g., nursing care, food service,
construction, agriculture). **Type 1 (1号)** allows several years of work; **Type 2 (2号)** allows
longer stays and family accompaniment in eligible fields.

## Business Manager (経営・管理)

For those who **establish or manage a business** in Japan, subject to capital, office, and business-
plan requirements.

## Choosing the Right Status

Your status must match what you actually do in Japan. Working outside your status, or staying past
your period, jeopardizes renewals and permanent residency. If your situation changes (new job type,
starting a business), apply for a **change of status** before you begin the new activity.
