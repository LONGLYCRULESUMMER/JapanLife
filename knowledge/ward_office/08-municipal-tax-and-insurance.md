# Paying Resident Tax and Insurance Through the Municipality

This page is about the **ward-office side** of local bills — how and when they arrive and how to
pay them. For how **resident tax is calculated**, see the tax-domain resident-tax guide; this guide
is about the **administrative procedure** at the municipal office.

## What the Municipality Bills You For
Registering on the resident record (住民票) makes you liable, where applicable, for:
- **Resident tax (住民税)** — billed by the municipality from June, based on last year's income.
- **National Health Insurance (国民健康保険)** premiums — if not on employer insurance.
- **National Pension (国民年金)** contributions — collected by the Japan Pension Service.

## How Bills Arrive
- Employees usually have resident tax and social insurance **deducted from salary** (特別徴収).
- Self-employed and recently unemployed residents receive **payment slips (納付書)** by mail and
  pay at convenience stores, banks, or by direct debit (口座振替).

## Payment Methods at or via the Ward Office
- Convenience-store payment with the slip's barcode
- Direct debit set up at the ward office or bank
- Some municipalities accept smartphone QR / app payments (e.g. PayPay, line of municipal apps)

## If You Cannot Pay
Contact the ward office **before** the due date. Municipalities can offer installment plans or, for
NHI and pension, **reductions and exemptions** based on income. Ignoring bills can lead to late
fees and, eventually, seizure procedures.

## When You Move or Leave Japan
Settle outstanding municipal bills when you move out. If you leave Japan mid-year with resident tax
still owed, appoint a **tax representative (納税管理人)** at the ward office to pay on your behalf.
