# Disaster Preparedness and Emergencies (防災・緊急時)

Japan experiences earthquakes, typhoons, and heavy rain. A little preparation and knowing the basic
emergency steps make a big difference, especially for newcomers.

## Emergency Numbers

- **110** — Police.
- **119** — Fire and ambulance (救急車).

When you call, state **what happened** and **where you are** (address or nearby landmark). Simple
Japanese phrases help, and operators can sometimes arrange interpretation.

## Earthquakes and Typhoons

- In an earthquake: protect your head, stay away from windows, and do not rush outside.
- Your municipality designates **evacuation areas (避難所)** and publishes **hazard maps** showing
  flood and landslide risk — check the ones near your home.

## Emergency Alerts

- **J-Alert** and **area mail** push earthquake/tsunami and evacuation alerts to mobile phones.
- Multilingual apps help foreigners: **NHK World**, the JNTO **Safety tips** app, and **Safety tips
  for travelers**.

## Evacuation Preparation

- Keep an **emergency go-bag**: water, non-perishable food, flashlight, batteries, first aid,
  copies of documents, and some cash.
- Agree on a **family meeting point** and how to contact each other.

## Support for Foreign Residents

Many municipalities provide **multilingual disaster information** and distribute hazard maps; some
run foreign-resident consultation lines during disasters.

## Tips for Foreigners

1. **Find your nearest 避難所 now**, before you need it.
2. Keep your **residence card** and a little **cash** in your go-bag.
3. Install a **multilingual disaster alert app** and enable emergency notifications.
