# National Pension (国民年金)

## Who Must Join
Everyone aged **20 to 59** registered as a resident of Japan must join the public pension system,
**regardless of nationality**. If you are not in an employer's Employees' Pension (厚生年金), you
enroll in the **National Pension (国民年金)** at your ward or city office, usually together with
your moving-in notification.

## Contributions
- The National Pension contribution is a **flat monthly amount** (around ¥17,000, adjusted yearly).
- Students can apply for the **Student Payment Exception (学生納付特例)**.
- Low-income residents can apply for **contribution exemptions or reductions (免除・猶予)**.
Applying for an exemption is far better than simply not paying, because it preserves your record.

## What It Provides
- **Old-age pension** from age 65 (with a minimum contribution period).
- **Disability** and **survivors'** pensions in qualifying cases.

## Lump-Sum Withdrawal When Leaving Japan
Foreign residents who paid into the pension for at least 6 months and then leave Japan can claim a
**Lump-sum Withdrawal Payment (脱退一時金)** within **2 years** of leaving. Consider the
**Totalization Agreements** Japan has with some countries, which let you combine pension periods
instead of withdrawing.

## Keep Records Clean for Visa Reviews
Pension payment history is checked in **permanent residency** and some renewal applications.
Unpaid months without an exemption can hurt those applications, so enroll and pay or file an
exemption promptly.
