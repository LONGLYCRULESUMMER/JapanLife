# Moving In: Ward Office Procedures (転入届)

When you move to a new municipality in Japan, registering at the ward/city office is the gateway to
almost everything else — insurance, pension, banking, and your resident record.

## Moving-In Notification (転入届) — Within 14 Days

Within **14 days** of moving to a new municipality, submit a **moving-in notification (転入届)** at
the **ward or city office (区役所・市役所)**. Try to do the related errands the same day.

## What to Bring

- **Residence card(s) (在留カード)** for every moving family member.
- The **moving-out certificate (転出証明書)** from your previous Japanese municipality (if moving
  within Japan).
- **Passports** and your **My Number** notification/card.

## Resident Record (住民票)

After registration you can obtain a **resident certificate (住民票)**, which you need to open bank
accounts, sign phone/utility contracts, and complete many official procedures.

## Procedures Bundled at Move-In

Registering your address typically lets you complete, in the same visit:

- **National Health Insurance (国民健康保険)** enrollment, if you are not on employer insurance.
- **National Pension (国民年金)** registration.
- **Child allowance (児童手当)** application, if you have children.
- **My Number** address update.
- **Seal registration (印鑑登録)**, if you need a registered seal.

Registration is also the basis for **resident tax (住民税)**.

## Moving Within the Same City (転居届)

If you move **within the same municipality**, file a **moving-within-city notification (転居届)**
instead — still within **14 days**.

## Tips for Foreigners

1. Bring **everyone's residence cards** and the **転出証明書** from your old city.
2. Do **bank, phone, and utility** setup **after** you receive your 住民票.
3. Knock out insurance, pension, and child allowance in the **same visit**.
