# Resident Record (住民票)

## What the Resident Record Is
The resident record (住民票) is the municipal register of who lives at an address. After you file
your moving-in notification (転入届), you are added to it. From it you can obtain a **resident
certificate (住民票の写し)**, an official document used for many procedures.

## When You Need a Resident Certificate
- Opening a bank account or signing phone / utility contracts
- Buying or leasing a car (sometimes with a parking certificate)
- Employment paperwork and some visa applications
- Applying for licenses and public benefits

## How to Get One
- At the ward / city office counter
- At a convenience store kiosk using a **My Number Card** (コンビニ交付), often cheaper and
  available outside office hours
- By mail for people living elsewhere

A certificate typically costs around **¥300** per copy. You can request a version with or without
your My Number and household details — only include My Number when an organization specifically
requires it.

## Household and Contents
The record lists your name, address, date of birth, sex, the head of household (世帯主) and your
relationship to them, and your move-in date. Foreign residents also have nationality and residence
status recorded.

## Keep It Current
Always update the resident record by filing the right notification within **14 days** of moving;
an out-of-date address can block contracts and is checked in visa reviews.
