# Common Certificates (各種証明書)

Day-to-day life in Japan often requires official certificates from the ward/city office. Knowing
which one you need saves repeat trips.

## Residence Certificate (住民票)

Proof of your registered address and household. Needed to open bank accounts, sign phone and
utility contracts, buy a car, and for many official procedures. You can request one with or without
the My Number / household details shown.

## Seal Registration Certificate (印鑑登録証明書)

Proof that your registered seal (実印) is officially yours. Required for high-value contracts such
as buying a car or property. You first **register a seal (印鑑登録)** at the ward office, then can
issue the certificate.

## Tax Certificates (課税証明書 / 納税証明書)

- **Taxation certificate (課税証明書)** shows your assessed income and resident tax.
- **Tax payment certificate (納税証明書)** shows that you have paid.

These are commonly required for **visa renewals**, loans, and some benefits.

## Where and How to Get Them

- At the **ward/city office counter**, by **mail**, or
- From a **convenience-store kiosk** using a **My Number Card** (住民票, 印鑑証明, and tax
  certificates in many municipalities).

Each certificate costs a small fee (around **¥300**).

## Tips for Foreigners

1. **Visa renewals** usually need a **住民票 plus tax certificates** — get them together.
2. A **My Number Card** lets you print certificates at a convenience store, even outside office
   hours.
3. Check whether you need the version **with or without** My Number shown before ordering.
