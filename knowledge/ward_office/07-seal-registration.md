# Seal Registration (印鑑登録)

## Why Seals Matter in Japan
Many formal contracts in Japan are sealed with a personal stamp (印鑑 / はんこ) instead of a
signature. A **registered seal (実印)** plus its **seal registration certificate (印鑑登録証明書)**
is required for high-value actions such as buying a car or property, and some loan and notarial
procedures.

## Types of Seal
- **Mitomein (認印)**: an everyday, unregistered seal for parcels and routine forms.
- **Jitsuin (実印)**: a seal you have officially registered with your municipality.
- **Ginkoin (銀行印)**: a seal registered with a bank for account transactions.

## How to Register
1. Bring a suitable seal and photo ID (residence card / My Number Card) to your ward or city office.
2. Complete a seal registration application (印鑑登録申請).
3. Receive a **seal registration card (印鑑登録証)** or have the function added to your My Number
   Card.

Rules on seal size and shape vary by municipality, and mass-produced seals may be rejected for a
jitsuin.

## Getting the Certificate
Once registered, you can obtain a **seal registration certificate (印鑑登録証明書)** at the counter
or, with a My Number Card, at a convenience-store kiosk. Each copy costs roughly **¥300**.

## When You Move
Seal registration is **cancelled automatically when you move out** of the municipality. You must
register again at your new municipality.
